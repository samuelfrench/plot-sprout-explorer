Adult guide

Shelf-clue story coaching

Before the session

  • Print the bookmark prompts, take-home slips, and adult guide before the session.
  • Set the adult-led rule: writers use paper bookmarks, pencils, sketches, and short story words only.
  • Choose one bookmark format before writers begin so every child has one clear writing path.
  • Say that a bookmark can hold a setting, character, object, sequence, revision, or sensory clue for a made-up story.
  • Keep the session screen-free, quiet, and focused on one small bookmark that can start a later story.

Bookmark setup

  • Place blank bookmarks, pencils, and take-home slips where the adult can see every page.
  • Set six labeled piles: setting bookmark, character bookmark, object bookmark, sequence bookmark, revision bookmark, and sensory bookmark.
  • Use broad bookshop words only, such as shelf, corner, table, sign, window, counter, or reading chair.
  • Leave space on each bookmark for a title, one sketch, three short lines, and one story sentence.
  • Keep extra bookmarks in an adult folder so the writing surface stays paper-only and simple.

Shelf-clue story coaching

  • Ask for one broad shelf place first, then turn it into a made-up setting with a mood word.
  • Ask for one invented character role who is looking for, carrying, noticing, or sorting something.
  • Ask for one pretend object from the shelf area and give it a story job.
  • Ask for first, next, and finally lines when the writer needs a clear beginning path.
  • Ask the writer to improve one sentence by adding a clearer look, sound, pattern, or texture word.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Use invented names, role words, or blank labels for every person on the bookmark.
  • Use broad place words instead of named locations, room numbers, route details, schedules, or private routines.
  • Keep all bookmark writing fictional and paper-only; do not collect contact details or personal facts.
  • Keep finished bookmarks with the family adult, tutor, co-op adult, or classroom adult.
  • Check every take-home slip for identifying details before it leaves the adult-led table.

Family handoff

  • Send one finished bookmark and one blank take-home bookmark slip with each writer.
  • Tell family adults that five to nine quiet minutes is enough for one extra bookmark prompt.
  • Invite the family adult to ask for one shelf place, one invented character, and one pretend object.
  • Ask adults to praise one clear word, sketch, or sentence before suggesting one small revision.
  • Suggest keeping bookmarks together so the writer can reuse the strongest clue in a later printable story.

Reset

  • Collect unused bookmarks, slips, pencils, and broad place cards.
  • Check finished bookmarks for private details before they go home.
  • Recycle scrap pages that only hold warm-up marks.
  • Restock the adult folder with fresh blank bookmarks for the next offline session.

Use one paper bookmark at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Bookmark formats

Choose the paper bookmark rhythm

Setting Bookmark

Writers who need a small bookshop place before inventing action.

  1. Adult points to one broad shelf, table, window, or corner word on the bookmark.
  2. Writer chooses a made-up setting name without using a real shop name.
  3. Writer adds one mood word and one look detail to the bookmark.
  4. Writer writes one sentence showing where the story begins.

Character Bookmark

Kids who like inventing roles, questions, and small choices.

  1. Adult asks the writer to choose an invented bookshop role, such as finder, sorter, helper, or mapmaker.
  2. Writer gives the character one question or choice.
  3. Writer sketches one clue about what the character notices.
  4. Writer writes one sentence about what the character does first.

Object Bookmark

Families turning ordinary shelf ideas into useful story objects.

  1. Adult asks the writer to invent one shelf object or choose one from a printed idea list.
  2. Writer gives the object one story job, such as guiding, hiding, reminding, or sorting.
  3. Writer adds one detail about how the object looks, feels, or sounds.
  4. Writer writes one sentence showing why the object matters.

Sequence Bookmark

Writers who need a tiny beginning, middle, and ending path.

  1. Adult points to the first, next, and finally lines on the bookmark.
  2. Writer chooses one shelf place and one invented character.
  3. Writer fills the three lines with calm story steps.
  4. Writer circles the step that should become the first full paragraph.

Revision Bookmark

Writers strengthening one sentence from an earlier bookmark or draft.

  1. Adult places the earlier sentence beside a blank revision bookmark.
  2. Writer chooses one clearer look, sound, pattern, or feeling word.
  3. Writer copies the sentence and adds the clearer detail.
  4. Writer underlines the word or phrase that improved the sentence most.

Sensory Bookmark

Quiet writers who notice color, sound, shape, pattern, or texture words.

  1. Adult asks the writer to choose one gentle sensory word from the paper prompt list.
  2. Writer pairs that word with a shelf place, object, or invented character.
  3. Writer sketches the detail in the bookmark corner.
  4. Writer writes one sentence where the sensory detail becomes a story clue.
