Tutor setup

Run the tutoring sprints

Before session

  1. Choose one sprint routine for the block and one backup slip for a shorter finish.
  2. Print only the pages needed for today's table so the packet stays light.
  3. Pick an invented setting, an ordinary object, and a small mix-up before the writer arrives.
  4. Place pencils, blank paper, and one quiet choice card within easy reach.
  5. Plan a short adult model that shows one clear detail and one next action.

Setup

  1. Seat the writer where the page, pencil, and timer are easy to see.
  2. Set the sprint page on top and keep take-home slips in a separate stack.
  3. Mark the first box to start so the writer does not need to scan every choice.
  4. Offer two invented options if the writer feels stuck at the opening.
  5. Keep the table clear except for the active page and one writing tool.

During sprint

  1. Start with a two-minute choice talk, then move quickly to marks on paper.
  2. Use invented people, places, and objects for every prompt.
  3. Prompt for one sentence, one sketch label, or one dictated phrase at a time.
  4. Praise a specific craft move such as a clearer verb, setting detail, or ordered step.
  5. Pause after each step and ask whether the writer wants the next choice or a quiet minute.

Wrap up

  1. Ask the writer to circle one finished part they want to keep.
  2. Choose one take-home slip that matches the skill practiced in the sprint.
  3. Write a simple finish mark such as title, detail, ending, or next step.
  4. Place loose pages in a plain folder or envelope for the family table.
  5. Reset unused slips and pencils before the next writer arrives.

No-data use

  1. Use invented details only; personal facts are not needed.
  2. Keep pages on paper with the tutor or family.
  3. Use color dots, symbols, or blank folders if pages need a simple mark.
  4. Share work only when the writer chooses a comfortable option.

Tutor tools

Sprint routines and optional share prompts

Ten-Minute Story Seed

Best for: A first tutoring block when the writer needs a fast win with little setup.

  1. Pick one invented place from two tutor options.
  2. Add one ordinary object that belongs in that place.
  3. Write three short lines: what is noticed, what changes, and what helps.
  4. Circle the strongest line and turn it into the opening of a tiny story.

Detail Ladder Sprint

Best for: Writers who make very short drafts and need more concrete description.

  1. Draw four ladder rungs on the page.
  2. Write one color, one sound, one texture, and one action on the rungs.
  3. Choose two rungs and place them in one sentence about an invented setting.
  4. Add one next action that makes the setting easier to picture.

Tiny Problem Turn

Best for: A middle sprint when the page has a setting but no movement yet.

  1. Choose a small mix-up such as a missing key, swapped map, or sleepy clock.
  2. Write what the story helper tries first.
  3. Write what the helper notices after the first try.
  4. Finish with one kind fix that changes the scene.

Quiet Dialogue Cards

Best for: Writers practicing conversation without a long draft.

  1. Label two invented roles such as maker and guide.
  2. Write one line where the maker asks for help.
  3. Write one line where the guide gives a useful clue.
  4. Add one sentence showing what both roles do next.

Ending Switch Sprint

Best for: A closing routine for revision, endings, and flexible thinking.

  1. Choose one draft sentence near the end of the page.
  2. Write a peaceful ending where the helper solves the mix-up.
  3. Write a second ending where the helper finds a new clue for later.
  4. Pick the ending that matches the beginning and add one clearer detail.

Optional share prompts

  • Sharing is optional: point to one word, sketch, or title you like.
  • You may show one circled detail and say why it belongs in the story.
  • You may ask the tutor to speak for your page, or you may pass.
  • Share one choice you changed during the sprint if you want to.
  • Show one ending choice with a thumbs-up, point, or quiet sentence.
  • Keep your page private today and listen for one clear detail from the group.
