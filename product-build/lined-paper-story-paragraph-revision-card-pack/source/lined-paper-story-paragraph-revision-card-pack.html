Adult guide

Story paragraph-revision coaching

Lined Paper Story Paragraph Revision Adult Guide

  • Print the lined-paper paragraph revision cards, blank lined sheets, and guide pages before the adult-led paper session.
  • Begin with one fictional draft paragraph, then coach a topic sentence, useful detail order, transition check, closing sentence, repeated-word cut, and final revised paragraph.
  • Keep every example made-up, broad, printable, offline, paper-only, and guided by an adult.
  • Use one lined sheet for the draft and one fresh lined sheet for the revised copy so changes stay easy to see.
  • Keep details inside the pretend story world; use invented names, simple objects, and broad settings instead of narrow facts.
  • End each activity by copying one final revised fictional paragraph with a clear topic sentence, ordered details, helpful transitions, and a closing sentence.

Use one lined-paper story paragraph-revision card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, paper-only, and screen-free.

Story paragraph-revision routines

Choose the lined-paper paragraph-revision routine

Topic Sentence Anchor

Making the first line tell what the fictional paragraph is mostly about.

  1. The adult points to the first line of the draft paragraph and asks for the main idea: ____________________.
  2. The writer underlines words that belong in the topic sentence: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which opening line would guide every detail on the lined page: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the revised topic sentence on the fresh lined sheet: ____________________.

Detail Order Ladder

Putting supporting details in an order that is easier to follow.

  1. The adult writes each fictional detail from the draft on a separate lined-paper strip: ____________________.
  2. The writer numbers the detail that should come first, second, and third: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which detail feels out of order or should move lower on the page: ____________________.
  4. The writer copies the details in the new order under the topic sentence: ____________________.

Transition Check Track

Adding small bridge words so the details connect on paper.

  1. The adult circles the spaces between detail sentences on the lined draft: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses a transition word or phrase for the first bridge: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks whether the next bridge shows time, place in the paragraph, or another idea: ____________________.
  4. The writer adds the transition words to the revised detail lines: ____________________.

Closing Sentence Wrap

Finishing the paragraph with one sentence that points back to the main idea.

  1. The adult reads the topic sentence and the ordered details aloud from the lined page: ____________________.
  2. The writer says what the paragraph helped the reader understand about the pretend moment: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which final sentence can wrap up the same main idea: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the closing sentence as the last line of the revised paragraph: ____________________.

Repeated Word Cut

Finding one overused word and replacing or removing it for smoother reading.

  1. The adult asks the writer to circle one word that appears too many times in the draft: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses one repeated word to keep because it is needed: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which repeated word can change, move, or come out: ____________________.
  4. The writer marks the repeated-word cut on the lined draft before copying: ____________________.

Final Revised Paragraph Copy

Copying the clean paragraph after each revision move is finished.

  1. The adult checks the revision marks in order: topic sentence, details, transitions, closing sentence, word cut: ____________________.
  2. The writer points to the first sentence that will be copied on the fresh lined sheet: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which sentence needs one last small pencil change before copying: ____________________.
  4. The writer copies the final revised fictional paragraph on fresh lined paper: ____________________.
