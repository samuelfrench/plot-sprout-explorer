Evidence coaching

Bookend Story Evidence Adult Guide: ____________________.

  • Set out one evidence card, six blank paper strips, and a pencil before the adult-led session begins: ____________________.
  • Label the strips story claim, first clue, second clue, because line, evidence sentence, and bookend note: ____________________.
  • Choose one pretend story world and keep every character, place, object, clue, and reason invented: ____________________.
  • Tell the writer that dictated words, adult-scribed words, or independent writing all fit the offline paper-only activity: ____________________.
  • Place the story claim strip at the left edge and the bookend note at the right edge so the invented evidence has a calm start and close: ____________________.

Facilitation notes

  • Start with a small story claim, not a big explanation: ____________________.
  • Ask for the first clue as one pretend detail the writer can point to on paper: ____________________.
  • Ask for the second clue as another invented detail that helps the same story claim: ____________________.
  • Use the because line to connect the two clues before asking for the evidence sentence: ____________________.
  • Close with the bookend note so the writer can name what the evidence helped show in the fictional story: ____________________.

Safety notes

  • Use invented characters, broad pretend places, and made-up clue details only: ____________________.
  • Keep the activity offline, paper-only, adult-led, and away from outside audiences: ____________________.
  • Skip any prompt that starts to ask for real names, real place details, or personal facts: ____________________.
  • Keep the evidence sentence about the pretend story, not about a real person or real event: ____________________.
  • Treat the bookend note as a private paper close for this session: ____________________.

Use one story evidence card at a time. Keep every evidence sentence broad, invented, paper-only, and guided by an adult.
