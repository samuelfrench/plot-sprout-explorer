Adult guide

Story paragraph-focus coaching

Clipboard Story Paragraph Focus Adult Guide

  • Print the clipboard paragraph focus cards, blank paragraph frames, and guide pages before the adult-led paper session.
  • Begin with one fictional paragraph plan, then coach a main idea, two useful details, detail order, one linking sentence, one cut idea, and a final focused copy.
  • Keep every example made-up, broad, printable, offline, paper-only, and guided by an adult.
  • Use small paper slips so the writer can move details before copying the final paragraph.
  • Replace narrow real facts with invented names, broad story places, and pretend objects before writing begins.
  • End each activity by copying one focused fictional paragraph that keeps one main idea and two helpful details.

Use one clipboard story paragraph-focus card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, paper-only, and screen-free.

Story paragraph-focus routines

Choose the clipboard paragraph-focus routine

Main Idea Clip

Choosing the one idea a fictional paragraph will stay about.

  1. The adult places one blank paragraph frame on the clipboard strip and writes the story topic: ____________________.
  2. The writer states the one main idea the paragraph should prove or show: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which words in the main idea must guide every sentence: ____________________.
  4. The writer copies the main idea line at the top of the paragraph frame: ____________________.

Detail Pair Sort

Choosing two useful details and leaving out loose ideas.

  1. The adult writes four invented detail notes on small paper slips: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses two details that clearly support the main idea: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which other detail is interesting but belongs outside this paragraph: ____________________.
  4. The writer places the detail pair under the main idea line: ____________________.

Detail Order Lineup

Putting two useful details in the clearest order.

  1. The adult sets the two chosen details side by side below the main idea: ____________________.
  2. The writer numbers the detail that should come first and the detail that should come second: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks why that order will help the reader follow the pretend moment: ____________________.
  4. The writer copies the ordered detail notes in the paragraph frame: ____________________.

Linking Sentence Bridge

Adding one sentence that connects the main idea to the details.

  1. The adult points from the main idea to the first detail and asks for one linking sentence: ____________________.
  2. The writer drafts a linking sentence that connects the main idea to detail one: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks whether the sentence points back to the same paragraph focus: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the linking sentence before the two detail lines: ____________________.

Extra Idea Lift

Cutting one interesting idea that pulls the paragraph away from its focus.

  1. The adult marks one sentence or note that wanders away from the main idea: ____________________.
  2. The writer explains why that extra idea can wait for another paper page: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which word or detail should stay after the extra idea comes out: ____________________.
  4. The writer rewrites the paragraph plan with the extra idea removed: ____________________.

Final Focus Copy

Copying one focused fictional paragraph after the plan is clean.

  1. The adult reads the paragraph plan in order: main idea, linking sentence, detail one, detail two: ____________________.
  2. The writer checks that every sentence supports the same focus: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks if one word should move, change, or come out before final copying: ____________________.
  4. The writer copies the final focused fictional paragraph on a fresh sheet: ____________________.
