Adult guide

Detail coaching

Folder Tab Detail Card Adult Guide

  • Print the detail cards, blank slips, and folder-tab labels before the adult-led paper session.
  • Use folder tabs as paper props for labeling, sorting, pointing, filing, comparing, and revising invented story details.
  • Keep every detail fictional, broad, offline, and guided by an adult.
  • Focus detail work on visual clues, story objects, gentle actions, and mood words.
  • Replace narrow outside facts with made-up tab labels before anyone writes.
  • Send along only blank slips or invented examples that use made-up people and broad pretend places.

Use one folder tab detail card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Detail routines

Choose the folder tab detail rhythm

Label the Detail

Writers who need one clear story detail before drafting.

  1. The adult places a blank folder tab beside one detail card.
  2. The writer chooses a broad tab label such as color, object, action, or mood.
  3. The adult asks which made-up detail belongs under that label.
  4. The writer writes one matching fictional detail: ____________________.

Sort Three Details

Choosing useful details without crowding a sentence.

  1. The adult lays out three blank slips and names one invented scene.
  2. The writer sorts slips into visual, object, and action tabs.
  3. The adult asks which tab gives the reader the clearest clue.
  4. The writer writes the chosen detail under the tab: ____________________.

Point to the Clue

Adding a detail that helps the reader notice one story clue.

  1. The adult uses a folder tab as a pointer beside a made-up story object.
  2. The writer names what the pointer helps the reader notice.
  3. The adult asks whether the clue is visual, action, object, or mood.
  4. The writer writes one pointed clue sentence: ____________________.

File by Mood

Connecting a gentle mood word to a visible story detail.

  1. The adult labels two tabs calm and curious.
  2. The writer chooses one invented object or action for each tab.
  3. The adult asks which tab better fits the next story moment.
  4. The writer files one mood detail into a sentence: ____________________.

Compare and Choose

Replacing a vague detail with a clearer fictional one.

  1. The adult places two invented detail slips under one folder tab.
  2. The writer compares which slip is easier to picture.
  3. The adult asks what makes the clearer detail useful.
  4. The writer writes the clearer revised detail: ____________________.

Revise the Tab

Changing a too-broad label into a stronger story-detail label.

  1. The adult reads the current folder-tab label aloud.
  2. The writer chooses a more specific invented label that stays broad and safe.
  3. The adult asks which detail now belongs under the revised label.
  4. The writer writes the revised tab detail: ____________________.
