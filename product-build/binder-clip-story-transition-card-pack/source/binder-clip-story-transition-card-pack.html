Adult guide

Transition coaching

Binder Clip Transition Card Adult Guide

  • Print the transition cards, blank slips, and guide before the adult-led paper session.
  • Use binder clips only as paper props for holding, grouping, or pointing to invented story moments.
  • Keep every story detail fictional, broad, offline, and guided by an adult.
  • Choose one before moment, one after moment, and one binder-clip job so the transition has a clear bridge.
  • Replace any personal place, schedule, group name, or child detail with a made-up story label.
  • Send along only blank slips or invented examples with no identity details.

Use one binder clip transition card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Transition routines

Choose the binder clip transition rhythm

Before-After Clip Bridge

Writers who need a clear bridge between two invented story moments.

  1. The adult names the pretend before moment and after moment from one card.
  2. The writer chooses a binder-clip job: hold, join, point, or flip.
  3. The adult asks what changes between the two pretend moments.
  4. The writer writes one bridge sentence for the transition: ____________________.

Meanwhile Clip Turn

Moving between two made-up scenes without adding outside facts.

  1. The adult places two slips side by side and clips a blank slip between them.
  2. The writer chooses a broad transition word such as meanwhile, later, or nearby.
  3. The adult asks what the reader needs to know before the next scene starts.
  4. The writer writes one fictional meanwhile sentence: ____________________.

Later Clip Stack

Showing a gentle passage from one story beat to the next.

  1. The adult stacks the before slip under the after slip with a binder clip.
  2. The writer names one made-up change that happened between the slips.
  3. The adult asks whether the change is small enough for one sentence.
  4. The writer writes one later sentence that explains the change: ____________________.

Object Thread

Using one invented object to connect two story moments.

  1. The adult points to the binder clip and asks what pretend object it can stand for.
  2. The writer chooses one broad object job such as marker, handle, tag, or hinge.
  3. The adult asks how the object appears in both story moments.
  4. The writer writes one transition using the object thread: ____________________.

Scene Swap Clip

Changing scenes while keeping places fictional and broad.

  1. The adult labels one slip old scene and one slip new scene.
  2. The writer chooses one invented place label with no narrow outside details.
  3. The adult asks what signal tells the reader the scene has changed.
  4. The writer writes one scene-swap transition sentence: ____________________.

Mood-to-Action Bridge

Connecting how an invented character feels to the next gentle action.

  1. The adult asks which pretend feeling starts the transition.
  2. The writer chooses one calm action that follows from that feeling.
  3. The adult clips the feeling slip to the action slip as a paper bridge.
  4. The writer writes one feeling-to-action transition: ____________________.
