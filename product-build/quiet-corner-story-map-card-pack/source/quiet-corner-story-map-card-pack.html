Adult guide

Story map coaching

Before the session

  • Print the story map cards, take-home map slips, and adult guide before writers arrive.
  • Set the adult-led rule: every idea stays offline, paper-only, and made up for creative writing.
  • Explain that each story map is a fictional story-shape card, not a tool for finding anything.
  • Choose one map routine before the session so each writer has a calm paper path.
  • Keep pencils, blank cards, and broad idea words ready for writers ages 6-11.

Quiet-corner setup

  • Place story map cards, blank paper, pencils, and take-home slips where the adult can see every page.
  • Set six labeled piles: setting map, character map, object map, sequence map, sensory map, and revision map.
  • Use broad pretend setting words such as nook, shelf, lamp, cushion, curtain, garden, bell, basket, or doorway.
  • Leave room on each card for a title, one small story shape, three labels, and one first line.
  • Keep extra cards in an adult folder so the quiet corner stays simple, offline, and paper-only.

Story map coaching

  • Ask for one pretend setting first, then turn it into a tiny story shape with one mood word.
  • Ask for one invented character role who notices, carries, sorts, waits, or changes something gentle.
  • Ask for one pretend object and give it a clear story job on the card.
  • Ask for first, next, and finally lines when the writer needs an easy paper path.
  • Ask the writer to revise one sentence by adding a clearer color, sound, label, pattern, or texture word.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Use invented labels, role words, or blank spaces for every person on the story map cards.
  • Use broad setting words instead of private details, named rooms, clubs, or narrow routines.
  • Keep finished story map cards with the family adult, tutor, co-op adult, or classroom adult.
  • Make sharing optional and limited to one invented word, small sketch, or short line.
  • Check every take-home map slip for private details before it leaves the adult-led table.

Family handoff

  • Send one finished story map card and one blank take-home map slip with each writer.
  • Tell family adults that five to nine quiet minutes is enough for one extra story map clue.
  • Invite the family adult to ask for one pretend setting, one invented character, and one pretend object.
  • Ask adults to praise one clear word, sketch, or sentence before suggesting one small revision.
  • Suggest keeping story map cards together so the writer can reuse the strongest paper clue in a later story.

Reset

  • Collect unused story map cards, take-home map slips, pencils, and broad idea cards.
  • Check finished story map cards for private details before they go with the family adult.
  • Recycle scrap pages that only hold warm-up marks.
  • Restock the adult folder with fresh story map cards for the next offline session.

Use one paper card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Map routines

Choose the paper map rhythm

Setting Map

Writers who need one pretend setting before adding story action.

  1. Adult points to one broad pretend setting word on the paper card.
  2. Writer draws a small story shape and labels the setting with a mood word.
  3. Writer adds one look detail that belongs inside the made-up quiet corner.
  4. Writer writes one first line showing where the story begins.

Character Map

Kids who like inventing roles, questions, and calm choices.

  1. Adult asks the writer to choose an invented role, such as keeper, finder, sorter, helper, or listener.
  2. Writer places the role beside the story shape on the card.
  3. Writer adds one question the character wonders about the pretend setting.
  4. Writer writes one sentence about what the character notices first.

Object Map

Families turning a simple paper clue into a useful story object.

  1. Adult asks the writer to invent one gentle object for the card.
  2. Writer gives the object one story job, such as guiding, reminding, hiding, sorting, or changing.
  3. Writer sketches the object beside the story shape and adds one label.
  4. Writer writes one sentence showing why the object matters.

Sequence Map

Writers who need a beginning, middle, and ending paper path.

  1. Adult points to the first, next, and finally spaces on the card.
  2. Writer chooses one pretend setting and one invented character.
  3. Writer fills the three spaces with calm story steps.
  4. Writer circles the step that should become the first full paragraph.

Sensory Map

Quiet writers who notice color, sound, shape, pattern, or texture words.

  1. Adult asks the writer to choose one gentle sensory word from the paper list.
  2. Writer pairs that word with the pretend setting, character, or object.
  3. Writer adds the sensory word as a label on the story shape.
  4. Writer writes one sentence where the sensory detail becomes a story clue.

Revision Map

Writers strengthening one sentence from an earlier story map card.

  1. Adult places the earlier sentence beside a blank revision map card.
  2. Writer chooses one clearer color, sound, label, pattern, or texture word.
  3. Writer copies the sentence and adds the clearer detail.
  4. Writer underlines the word or phrase that improved the sentence most.
