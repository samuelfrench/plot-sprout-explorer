Counselor setup

Run the story circles

Before camp

  1. Print one counselor copy, one circle-format page for the block, and one take-home trail card stack before the group arrives.
  2. Choose two invented camp settings such as pinecone pier, lantern lodge, or meadow mailbox for quick adult examples.
  3. Place blank paper, pencils, color dots, and symbol cards in a supply bin for simple printable sorting.
  4. Pick one quiet backup format for writers who need a smaller table or a silent start.
  5. Prepare a thirty-second adult model using an invented helper, an ordinary object, and a small friendly mix-up.

Setup

  1. Seat writers where they can see the adult leader and reach one page without passing a stack around the circle.
  2. Set out only the active circle page, pencils, and a few symbol cards so the table stays calm.
  3. Use color dots, sun symbols, leaf symbols, or blank folders if pages need to be grouped after the activity.
  4. Read the first direction aloud, then show one invented example before asking for ideas.
  5. Keep extra pages in a counselor folder and hand them out only when the group is ready for the next step.

Running circles

  1. Start with a short invented setting choice and repeat the choice so every writer hears the same story seed.
  2. Invite responses by pointing, drawing, dictating one phrase to the adult leader, or writing a short line.
  3. Move around the circle with one small job at a time: setting, helper, object, clue, then ending.
  4. Redirect personal facts by offering two invented options and saying the story can use make-believe details today.
  5. End by asking writers to mark one sentence, sketch, or title they want to keep.

Quiet options

  1. Offer a silent sketch-and-label page for writers who want to join without speaking.
  2. Let a writer point to a symbol card while the adult leader reads the matching invented option.
  3. Use a two-person table circle when the full group is too loud for careful writing.
  4. Give early finishers a trail card and ask them to add one invented clue or ending detail.
  5. Allow passing during share time while still letting the writer keep, fold, or file the page.

No-data use

  1. Use invented places, invented helpers, and ordinary objects; personal facts are not needed.
  2. Keep all pages printable and handled by the adult leader, family, or participant.
  3. Use color dots, symbols, or blank folders for simple sorting instead of private labels.
  4. Sharing stays optional and can be done by pointing to a word, sketch, title, or chosen sentence.

Circle formats and optional share prompts

Pinecone Pass-Around

Best for: A first camp circle when the group needs one shared idea and quick turns.

  1. Adult leader starts with an invented place such as Pinecone Pier and says one peaceful detail.
  2. Each writer adds one ordinary object, sound, color, or tiny clue to the shared story seed.
  3. Adult leader repeats the gathered ideas and asks writers to choose two for their own page.
  4. Writers draft a short opening where a friendly helper notices the chosen details.

Cabin Card Chain

Best for: Small groups that enjoy building a story in ordered steps.

  1. Adult leader places four cards in the center: place, helper, object, and ending.
  2. The circle fills each card with an invented detail using quick spoken, pointed, or written choices.
  3. Writers number the cards first, next, then, and last on their own page.
  4. Each writer turns the card chain into four short story lines.

Quiet Trail Map

Best for: A shaded-table option for mixed-energy groups or hot afternoons.

  1. Adult leader gives each writer a simple path shape with three blank trail stops.
  2. Writers label the stops with an invented camp place, an object clue, and a kind fix.
  3. Adult leader offers two example verbs for anyone who needs a starting word.
  4. Writers add one sentence under each stop to make a tiny trail story.

Lantern Lodge Listening Circle

Best for: Groups practicing listening before they write.

  1. Adult leader reads a two-sentence invented scene about Lantern Lodge and a misplaced bell.
  2. Writers listen for one setting detail, one object, and one possible helper.
  3. The circle shares optional details while the adult leader writes neutral choices on a visible page.
  4. Writers choose one detail and write what the helper does next.

Meadow Mail Reply

Best for: A letter-style circle for recreation rooms, picnic tables, or co-op groups.

  1. Adult leader announces that a note arrived from an invented place called Meadow Mail Tent.
  2. The group chooses what small friendly problem the note describes.
  3. Writers draft a three-line reply with one greeting, one useful idea, and one closing action.
  4. Adult leader invites writers to underline the clearest action line before cleanup.

Four-Beat Lantern Tale

Best for: A camp-themed story circle that avoids unsafe activity instructions while keeping the evening feel.

  1. Adult leader draws four boxes labeled glow, clue, helper, and home base.
  2. Writers fill each box with invented details such as a jar lantern, ribbon map, or pebble path.
  3. The circle quietly points to favorite box ideas and the adult leader names two possible orders.
  4. Writers write one sentence per box and finish with a calm return to the home base.

Optional share prompts

  • Sharing is optional: point to one word, sketch, or title you want the group to notice.
  • Read one sentence that shows an invented setting detail, or ask the adult leader to read it.
  • Show one object clue and say how it helps the story if you want to.
  • Share one kind fix your helper tried in the invented story.
  • Point to the strongest action word on your page.
  • Tell the circle which trail step came first, next, then, or last.
  • Show a title choice and explain which story clue it gives.
  • Pass today and listen for one clear detail from another writer's invented story.
