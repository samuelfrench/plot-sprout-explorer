Adult guide

Show-not-tell coaching

Index Card Show-Not-Tell Adult Guide

  • Print the index cards, blank clue rows, and revision slips before the adult-led paper session.
  • Begin with one plain line and coach the writer to show it through visible clue, object action, place signal, character gesture, and paper-safe sound or texture detail.
  • Keep every example fictional, broad, offline, and guided by an adult.
  • Use one card per sentence so the page stays small enough for ages 7-11.
  • Replace any actual person detail or narrow outside fact with a made-up name, broad story place, or pretend object.
  • End each card with a revised sentence that shows the idea without naming it too directly.

Use one index card show-not-tell card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Show-not-tell routines

Choose the index card show-not-tell rhythm

Plain Line Clue Row

Writers who tell the idea first and need a simple path toward showing it.

  1. The adult reads one plain line from an index card.
  2. The writer names the idea the line currently tells.
  3. The adult points to five clue slots: visible clue, object action, place signal, character gesture, and sound/texture detail.
  4. The writer writes one revised sentence using two clue slots: ____________________.

Object Action Swap

Turning a flat sentence into a sentence with a visible story object.

  1. The adult circles the object in a plain line.
  2. The writer chooses one gentle action the object can do, such as lean, tap, wobble, or slide.
  3. The adult asks what the object action helps the reader notice.
  4. The writer writes one object action sentence: ____________________.

Place Signal Pass

Showing a broad fictional place without naming it too plainly.

  1. The adult covers the direct place word in a plain line.
  2. The writer chooses a place signal such as an arch, shelf, banner, puddle, or lantern.
  3. The adult asks what the signal tells the reader about the pretend place.
  4. The writer writes one place signal sentence: ____________________.

Gesture Before Feeling

Replacing a named feeling with a character gesture the reader can see.

  1. The adult reads a plain line that tells how a fictional character feels.
  2. The writer chooses one character gesture, such as toe tapping, sleeve smoothing, or eyebrow lifting.
  3. The adult asks which feeling the gesture lets the reader infer.
  4. The writer writes one gesture-first sentence: ____________________.

Sound Texture Thread

Adding one paper-safe sound or texture detail to make the moment easier to imagine.

  1. The adult rereads the plain line and asks where a tiny sensory clue could fit.
  2. The writer chooses one sound word or texture phrase a reader can imagine from print.
  3. The adult asks whether the clue supports the visible action instead of distracting from it.
  4. The writer writes one sound or texture detail sentence: ____________________.

Revised Sentence Stack

Combining the strongest clues into one clear revised sentence.

  1. The adult places the plain line at the top of the index card stack.
  2. The writer chooses the strongest visible clue and one supporting clue.
  3. The adult asks which telling word can be removed.
  4. The writer writes the final revised sentence: ____________________.
