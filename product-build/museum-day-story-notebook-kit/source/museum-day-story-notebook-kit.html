Adult guide

Use the notebook day

Before visit

  • Print one adult copy, the planned notebook pages, and a small stack of take-home observation cards before the group arrives.
  • Choose one calm gallery focus such as shape, pattern, object use, texture, or setting detail for the first writing table.
  • Prepare pencils, clipboards, plain folders, and a quiet choice bank so the activity can run without devices.
  • Review any venue rules with the adult team and keep the writing task at tables, benches, or approved education spaces.
  • Plan a short adult model that turns one observed detail into an invented story clue without using personal facts.

Table setup

  • Place only the active notebook page, pencil, and one choice strip at each seat so the table stays uncluttered.
  • Keep extra cards, spare pages, and folders in an adult supply stack until the group is ready for them.
  • Use color tabs, symbols, or blank folders for simple sorting instead of private identifiers.
  • Set a quiet table option where participants can sketch, point, or write one short line before joining a larger task.
  • Leave clear space for clipboards and printed pages so the writing table works in a museum, gallery, co-op, or library room.

Observation to story

  • Start with one visible detail such as a color, material, shape, shadow, pattern, label-free object, or room feature.
  • Ask writers to turn that detail into an invented setting, helper, object clue, or small friendly mix-up.
  • Offer two adult-created options when someone is stuck, and keep every story detail make-believe.
  • Move from observation to fiction in small steps: notice, choose, imagine, write, and mark one detail to keep.
  • Use neutral language such as maker, guide, visitor, collector, builder, or messenger for invented roles.

Quiet participation

  • Allow participation by pointing to a choice, circling a detail, sketching a label, dictating one phrase, or writing privately.
  • Let anyone pass during sharing while still completing a notebook page or take-home card.
  • Offer a silent five-minute observation card when the room is busy or the group needs a settled reset.
  • Invite partners to share only a title, object clue, or setting detail instead of a full draft.
  • Keep adult support focused on the printed prompt, the observed detail, and the next small story step.

No-data use

  • This kit uses no personal lists, personal labels, digital collection, image collection, or web-sharing step.
  • Use invented places, invented roles, and ordinary objects rather than personal facts.
  • Keep every page offline, printable, and handled by the adult leader, participant, or family.
  • Organize pages with symbols, color tabs, or plain folders instead of private identifiers.

Family handoff

  • Send home the notebook page, one optional observation card, and a note that the story details are invented.
  • Mark one simple next step such as add a title, add one detail, or finish the ending.
  • Tell families the card can be completed by pointing, sketching, dictating, or writing one short line.
  • Keep any sharing optional and focused on a printed page, sketch, title, object clue, or invented setting.

Use these pages offline for adult-guided museum days. Keep finished pages in folders or send them home.

Visit tools

Visit formats and optional family-share prompts

One-Room Detail Hunt

Best for: Short family visits, co-op gallery blocks, or museum educator tables that need one focused observation loop.

  1. Adult leader chooses one room or display area and names the focus: shape, color, pattern, material, or object use.
  2. Participants silently notice one detail and mark it on the printed notebook page.
  3. Adult leader models how that detail could become an invented story clue.
  4. Writers draft a short setting or object line before moving to the next page.

Table-First Museum Writing

Best for: Education rooms, gallery tables, library museum days, or groups that start seated before viewing.

  1. Adult leader gives each writer a choice bank with invented roles and ordinary objects.
  2. Writers pick one role and one object before observing a display or printed image set.
  3. Adult leader asks for one visible detail that can connect to the chosen object.
  4. Writers combine the three choices into a two-sentence story start.

Sketch Then Sentence

Best for: Quiet participants, mixed-age family groups, and busy gallery moments where speaking is optional.

  1. Adult leader asks writers to sketch one safe visible shape or pattern on the notebook page.
  2. Writers label the sketch with one describing word or adult-offered choice.
  3. Adult leader shows how the label can become an invented place, clue, or helper job.
  4. Writers add one sentence that uses the sketch without copying any private information.

Object Clue Circle

Best for: Small groups with a museum educator, co-op adult, or family leader guiding a shared table.

  1. Adult leader places four ordinary object cards in the center of the table.
  2. Participants choose one object card and connect it to one observed color, shape, or texture.
  3. Adult leader invites optional pointing or short spoken choices while recording only invented options.
  4. Writers turn the object and detail into one friendly story problem and one next action.

Three-Stop Notebook Path

Best for: Longer field-trip table blocks where adults can guide three approved stops without rushing.

  1. Adult leader marks three approved stops: a detail stop, an object stop, and a story ending stop.
  2. Writers add one note or sketch at each stop on the same notebook page.
  3. Adult leader helps writers order the three notes as first, next, and last.
  4. Writers draft a tiny story path and underline the clearest detail.

Exit Table Wrap-Up

Best for: The final 10 minutes of a museum day, gallery lesson, or family learning visit.

  1. Adult leader asks writers to stop at the end of the line or sketch they are working on.
  2. Writers mark one observed detail and one invented story idea they want to keep.
  3. Adult leader offers one take-home observation card that matches the marked idea.
  4. Pages go into plain folders before the table is reset for the next group.

Optional share prompts

  • Show one sketch, title, or observed detail from the notebook page.
  • Read one invented sentence aloud, or ask a grown-up to read it.
  • Point to one object clue and explain how it helps the story.
  • Share one setting detail that came from a shape, color, pattern, or material.
  • Tell which story step came first, next, or last.
  • Show one word you made clearer after the visit.
  • Describe one calm ending choice from the invented story.
  • Pass today and keep the page private while the family notices one finished detail.
