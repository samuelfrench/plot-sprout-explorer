Adult guide

Sequence move coaching

Before the session

  • Print and cut the desk drawer sequence cards before the writer arrives.
  • Choose six to ten cards for the group size and place the rest aside.
  • Set blank paper, pencils, blank slips, and a small envelope or box to stand in for the pretend drawer.
  • Explain that the drawer objects are fictional paper clues used to build a made-up story order.
  • Start with one adult example so writers see how beginning, middle, and ending cards line up.

Paper sequence setup

  • Place the selected cards face down in a small envelope, tray, or pretend desk drawer.
  • Pull single cards and set each into one of three zones: beginning, middle, or ending.
  • Let writers move cards before writing so the sequence can change without erasing.
  • Keep blank bridge slips nearby for one missing event between two cards.
  • Save skipped cards in a later pile so the session keeps moving.

Story sequence coaching

  • Ask what has to happen before this card can make sense.
  • Use concrete transition words: first, next, then, after that, because, and finally.
  • When a writer stalls, offer two possible spots for the card and let the writer choose.
  • Point to the paper order before asking for another sentence.
  • Finish with one quick retell from left to right before the final writing round.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Keep all work on paper and offline with the adult leader or family folder.
  • Use invented names, pretend places, and made-up drawer objects.
  • Redirect any personal or narrow real-life detail into a broad invented detail before writing.
  • A writer may point to one card or share one invented word instead of reading aloud.
  • Skip any prompt that stops feeling gentle, invented, paper-only, or adult-led.

Family handoff

  • Send one ordered card row, one blank bridge slip, or one short fiction sentence.
  • Tell family adults the task is event order practice, not a mark of writing ability.
  • Invite one gentle paper retell using first, next, then, and finally.
  • Ask families to keep examples fictional and offline.
  • Return unfinished cards in a folder for the next adult-led session.

Reset

  • Collect selected cards, blank slips, and draft pages.
  • Return finished pages to the writer or adult folder and recycle only scrap warm-ups.
  • Sort cards by beginning, middle, ending, cause, and transition focus if useful.
  • Shuffle the drawer stack before the next session.

Use one paper sequence card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Sequence routines

Choose the paper sequence rhythm

Drawer Pull Warm-Up

A gentle opening round for one writer or a small table.

  1. Pull three cards from the pretend drawer.
  2. Read each card aloud and place them in any order.
  3. Ask which card has to happen first.
  4. Write one sentence for each card using first, next, and then.

Beginning Middle Ending Sort

Writers who need a clear story structure before drafting.

  1. Draw six cards and spread them across the table.
  2. Choose two cards for the beginning zone.
  3. Choose two cards for the middle zone.
  4. Choose two cards for the ending zone and retell the row aloud.

Cause Card Chain

Practicing why one event leads to another.

  1. Pull four cards and choose one card to start the chain.
  2. Place the next card only after saying what caused it.
  3. Move any card that does not connect yet to a later spot.
  4. Write the sequence as four short story beats.

Missing Bridge Slip

Fixing a story that skips ahead too quickly.

  1. Read the ordered cards from left to right.
  2. Stop where the story feels like it skipped a step.
  3. Have the adult write one fictional bridge event on a blank slip.
  4. Place the bridge slip in the gap and retell the sequence once.

Setting Path Row

Showing how a character moves through a pretend drawer world.

  1. Pick three fictional drawer spots such as tab corner, paperclip hill, or stamp stack.
  2. Place a card at each setting spot.
  3. Decide which spot the character visits first, next, and last.
  4. Write one sentence that shows the move between each spot.

Ending Drawer Swap

Revising an ending without rewriting the full story.

  1. Keep the beginning and middle cards in their current order.
  2. Pull two new ending cards from the pretend drawer.
  3. Compare which ending fits the character's earlier choice.
  4. Write the final two sentences using the chosen ending card.
