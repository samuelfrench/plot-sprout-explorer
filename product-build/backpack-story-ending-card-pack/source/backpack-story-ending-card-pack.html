Adult guide

Ending coaching

Before the session

  • Print the ending cards, backpack sleeve, blank slips, and adult guide before writers gather.
  • Use a pretend paper backpack only; no real bags, labels, or private items are part of the activity.
  • Set out pencils and scrap paper for fictional closing choices, final feelings, object returns, echo lines, and final images.
  • Tell writers every ending belongs to an invented story world and can be changed gently.
  • Keep the session adult-led, offline, and paper-only from the first card to the last slip.

Backpack ending setup

  • Place three ending cards in the pretend paper backpack and keep the rest face down.
  • Put one blank slip beside each card for the writer's chosen closing detail.
  • Invite the writer to pick one ending job: return an object, echo an earlier line, choose a final feeling, or draw the last image.
  • Ask the adult helper to read choices aloud and let the writer select one paper path.
  • Keep every label invented, broad, and tied to the story page rather than personal life.

Story ending coaching

  • Ask what choice the character makes on the final page and write it in one short phrase.
  • Ask which pretend object comes back and what it means now.
  • Help the writer echo one earlier story word without copying a whole scene.
  • Name one final feeling that is calm, clear, or hopeful.
  • Use gentle revision by asking what can be clearer, softer, or more complete on the ending slip.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Keep the activity on invented story words, broad pretend places, and paper-only choices.
  • If a writer mentions a narrow real-world fact, replace it with an invented story label.
  • Keep completed slips with the adult helper or send one paper slip to a family reader.
  • Use only fictional characters, pretend objects, broad story places, and made-up closing lines.
  • Skip any prompt that feels like collecting private details instead of shaping a fictional ending.

Family handoff

  • Send one chosen ending slip with a note that the activity stays adult-led and offline.
  • Invite the family reader to ask about the character's final choice, not about private life.
  • Offer one fresh blank slip for a gentler alternate ending.
  • Tell families that invented labels and made-up places are the safest way to keep the story playful.
  • Let the writer decide whether to read a line aloud or simply point to the final image.

Reset

  • Collect the cards, blank slips, pencils, and paper backpack sleeve.
  • Remove any slip with narrow real-world facts and rewrite it as an invented story detail if the adult keeps it.
  • Shuffle ending cards so the next group sees a fresh mix.
  • Store finished fictional slips separately from unused blanks.

Use one backpack ending card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

ending routines

Choose the backpack ending rhythm

Object Comes Back

Writers who need a concrete way to close the story.

  1. Adult chooses one ending card and asks which pretend object mattered earlier.
  2. Writer writes how the object appears again near the end.
  3. Adult asks what the object means after the character's final choice.
  4. Writer writes one closing sentence that returns the object gently.

Echo Line Close

Writers practicing a satisfying repeated phrase.

  1. Adult helps the writer pick one harmless word or short phrase from earlier in the story.
  2. Writer changes the phrase slightly so it feels new at the end.
  3. Adult asks what feeling the echo should leave.
  4. Writer writes the echo as the last spoken or narrated line.

Final Feeling Fold

Ending a story with emotional clarity.

  1. Adult places three feeling slips beside one ending card.
  2. Writer picks one gentle final feeling for the character.
  3. Adult asks what choice helped the character reach that feeling.
  4. Writer writes one sentence that shows the feeling through action.

Last Image Window

Writers who think in pictures before sentences.

  1. Adult asks the writer to sketch or name the final image on a blank slip.
  2. Writer adds one color, sound, or tiny motion to the image.
  3. Adult asks why that image belongs at the end.
  4. Writer turns the image into one closing sentence.

Choice And Consequence

Showing that an ending follows from a character decision.

  1. Adult asks what the character finally chooses to do.
  2. Writer writes the choice in one short invented sentence.
  3. Adult asks what small, gentle change happens because of the choice.
  4. Writer writes the change as the next-to-last or last sentence.

Gentle Ending Repair

Revising an ending that feels abrupt or unclear.

  1. Adult reads the ending card and asks which part feels unfinished.
  2. Writer marks one place to add a closing choice, object return, feeling, or image.
  3. Adult offers two calm wording options and lets the writer choose.
  4. Writer writes a cleaner final line on a fresh slip.
