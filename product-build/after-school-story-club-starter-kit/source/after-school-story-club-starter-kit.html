Director setup

Run the story club

Before club

  1. Print one director copy, one active club format, and enough take-home prompt cards for the planned group size.
  2. Choose a calm invented setting, an ordinary object, and a small friendly mix-up for the adult example.
  3. Place pencils, blank paper, choice slips, and plain folders in a single supply bin before the room opens.
  4. Pick one main format and one quieter backup format so the session can adjust without extra prep.
  5. Prepare a thirty-second adult model that uses only invented details and shows one clear next action.

Room setup

  1. Set tables so writers can see the adult leader, reach supplies, and keep their pages on the table.
  2. Put only the active page, pencils, and a small stack of blank paper within reach at the start.
  3. Keep take-home prompt cards in a separate folder until the last few minutes of club.
  4. Use table symbols, color dots, or plain folders if pages need simple sorting after the session.
  5. Create one quiet corner with the same writing page for participants who prefer a lower-noise start.

Running club

  1. Open with the session choice, read the first direction aloud, and show one invented adult example.
  2. Move through one task at a time: setting, helper, object, small mix-up, next action, and ending.
  3. Offer two invented options when a writer is stuck instead of asking for personal facts.
  4. Invite participation through writing, sketch labels, pointing to a choice, or dictating one phrase to the adult leader.
  5. End by asking each writer to mark one title, sentence, sketch, or detail they want to keep.

Quiet participation

  1. Allow passing during discussion while still giving the writer a page and a clear private task.
  2. Use silent choice slips so a participant can point to setting, object, or ending options without speaking.
  3. Let writers circle, underline, or sketch a response when full sentences feel too large for the moment.
  4. Pair a quiet writer with the adult leader for one dictated phrase if the writer wants support.
  5. Offer a take-home prompt card as the finish step for anyone who completes the club page early.

No-data use

  1. Do not collect personal details; all stories use invented places, invented helpers, and ordinary objects.
  2. This kit has no personal lists, personal labels, digital collection, image collection, or web-sharing step.
  3. Keep every page printable, offline, and handled by the adult leader, participant, or family.
  4. Use symbols, color dots, or plain folders for organization instead of private identifiers.

Family handoff

  1. Send home the finished page, one optional prompt card, and a short note that the story details are invented.
  2. Tell families the prompt card is optional and can be completed with pointing, sketching, or one sentence.
  3. Mark one simple next step such as add a title, add one clearer detail, or finish the ending.
  4. Keep any group sharing optional and focused on a title, sketch, object, or invented setting detail.

Director tools

Club formats and optional family-share prompts

Snack Table Story Seed

Best for: The first 15 minutes of an after-school block when arrivals are uneven and the room needs a calm start.

  1. Adult leader places one seed page and two invented setting choices at each seat.
  2. Writers choose a setting, add one ordinary object, and sketch or write one detail.
  3. Adult leader models a short opening using a different invented choice.
  4. Writers turn their setting and object into two or three story lines.

Homework-Finished Mini Quest

Best for: Mixed groups where some writers need a quiet enrichment option after other table work is done.

  1. Adult leader gives a mini quest page with four boxes labeled place, helper, object, and ending.
  2. Writers fill each box with invented details from the printed choice bank.
  3. Adult leader checks that each page has one small friendly mix-up and one helpful next action.
  4. Writers draft one sentence per box and choose a title before cleanup.

Community Room Story Circle

Best for: Community centers, childcare site leads, and co-op adults running one shared table activity.

  1. Adult leader reads a two-sentence invented scene and places four choice slips in the center.
  2. Participants add ideas by speaking, pointing, sketching, or writing on their own page.
  3. Adult leader repeats the shared idea using neutral invented details only.
  4. Writers choose two shared details and make a short story start on their own page.

Quiet Folder Finish

Best for: The final 10 minutes when the group needs a settled ending and easy family handoff.

  1. Adult leader asks writers to stop at the end of the line or sketch part they are working on.
  2. Writers mark one part to keep and one part that could use a title, detail, or ending.
  3. Adult leader offers one take-home prompt card that matches the marked next step.
  4. Pages go into plain folders before supplies are reset for the next club day.

Four-Corner Story Builder

Best for: Rooms with extra table space and writers who benefit from moving through clear stations.

  1. Adult leader sets four corners: invented place, helper, ordinary object, and kind ending.
  2. Writers visit each corner and add one note to their own page.
  3. Adult leader brings everyone back to seats and models how to order the four notes.
  4. Writers draft a tiny story using first, next, then, and last.

Co-Op Choice Board

Best for: Homeschool co-op adults who want a flexible adult-led writing block for different ages.

  1. Adult leader displays three printable choices: map, message, or object clue.
  2. Writers choose one path and complete the matching first box.
  3. Adult leader offers short support prompts while keeping all details invented.
  4. Writers finish with one ending line and one optional share choice.

Optional share prompts

  • Show one title, sketch, or invented setting detail from the club page.
  • Read one sentence aloud, or ask a grown-up to read it for you.
  • Point to one ordinary object and explain how it helps the story.
  • Share one small next action the helper could take.
  • Tell which part came first, next, or last in the story.
  • Show one word you made clearer after club.
  • Describe one kind ending choice from the invented story.
  • Pass today and keep the page private while the family notices one finished detail.
