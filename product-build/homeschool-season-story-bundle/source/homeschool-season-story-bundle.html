Adult setup guide

Run the year in small sessions

Setup

  1. Print one season at a time so the year feels manageable instead of like a workbook stack.
  2. Pick one weekly writing slot and keep each quest to a short, visible finish line.
  3. Use the pages offline for private family, tutoring, or classroom writing practice.
  4. Save each finished quest behind a season divider to build a concrete writing portfolio.

Season plan

  1. Fall: Collect concrete setting details, map small errands, and explain one helpful change.
  2. Winter: Practice clear sequence, concise labels, and polite dialogue before drafting.
  3. Spring: Observe carefully, choose clearer words, and revise one sentence at a time.
  4. Summer: Use cause-and-effect notes, read aloud one favorite line, and finish with a kind ending.

Support moves

  • Let the child point, circle, sketch, or dictate before asking for full sentences.
  • Offer two choices when a blank feels too open, then let the child pick and copy one phrase.
  • Read back one strong sentence and ask what single detail would make it clearer.
  • Stop after one finished quest page rather than stretching the session into extra pages.
  • Praise a concrete writing move such as a useful map label, careful observation, or polite line of speech.

Extension ideas

  • Staple three quest pages into a seasonal mini-book with a hand-drawn cover.
  • Let siblings or co-op partners complete different pages and trade read-aloud moments.
  • Choose one sentence each month for a gentle revision pass with a brighter verb or clearer detail.
  • Use the portfolio folder at the end of the year to pick the child's strongest story scene.
  • Turn one favorite quest into a family read-aloud script with narrator and helper roles.
