Adult setup

Run the story club

Setup

  1. Print one session packet per participant plus a few spare draft pages, then stack each packet in use order before the group arrives.
  2. Choose one club routine and one extension activity in advance so the session does not depend on last-minute sorting.
  3. Place pencils, plain paper, and choice slips in the center of each table before writers sit down.
  4. Use table numbers or simple symbols only if labels are needed for folders.
  5. Open with a brief adult model that shows one setting detail, one helpful choice, and one clear ending.

Group norms

  1. Everyone may write, draw, point to a choice, or dictate one sentence to the adult leader.
  2. Listen quietly when someone shares and respond to the writing, not to personal details.
  3. Keep stories fictional and use invented places, invented characters, and ordinary objects.
  4. Pass is always allowed during sharing; a listener role still counts as participation.
  5. Ask for help by pointing to a prompt, underlining a stuck spot, or choosing between two adult-offered options.

Materials

  1. Printed club packets with session pages, draft pages, extension pages, and optional share prompts.
  2. Sharpened pencils, erasers, colored pencils, sticky notes, plain folders, and one timer visible to the adult leader.
  3. A small table supply bin for each group with only quiet materials that can be cleaned up quickly.
  4. Blank index-size slips for title tests, detail banks, and next-step notes.
  5. A leader copy with the timing plan, group norms, and backup extension activities clipped together.

Timing

  1. 30-minute club: 5 minutes for welcome and norms, 7 minutes for story choices, 12 minutes for drafting, 4 minutes for quiet revision, and 2 minutes for cleanup.
  2. 45-minute club: 5 minutes for setup, 10 minutes for adult-led prompt launch, 15 minutes for drafting, 8 minutes for extension work, and 7 minutes for optional sharing.
  3. 60-minute club: 8 minutes for warm-up, 12 minutes for routine steps, 20 minutes for writing, 10 minutes for extension, 5 minutes for share prompts, and 5 minutes for take-home reset.
  4. Drop-in version: run a 10-minute detail bank, a 10-minute mini draft, and a 5-minute folder finish so participants can leave at natural breaks.
  5. Tutoring version: use 5 minutes for choice talk, 15 minutes for guided writing, 10 minutes for revision, and 5 minutes for reading one sentence back.

Take-home

  1. Send pages home in a plain folder with one clear finish job marked by the adult leader.
  2. Keep unfinished work private unless the participant chooses to share during the session.
  3. Include one optional family prompt that asks for an invented setting, object, or ending rather than personal information.
  4. Store leftover blank pages with the club supplies so the next session can begin without reprinting the whole kit.

Club tools

Routines and optional share prompts

Welcome Table Detail Bank

Best for: The first 10 minutes of a library, co-op, classroom, or tutoring club when arrivals are uneven.

  1. Adult places one draft page and one detail-bank slip at each seat before the session begins.
  2. Writers list or sketch one color, one object, one sound, and one quiet action from an invented setting.
  3. Adult circulates and offers two simple choices to anyone who is stuck.
  4. Each writer circles one detail to carry into the main story prompt.

Whole-Group Story Launch

Best for: Mixed-age groups that need a shared start before independent writing.

  1. Adult reads the session prompt aloud and shows two printed choice slips for the setting.
  2. The group chooses by pointing, raising a hand, or quietly naming one option.
  3. Adult writes the shared choice on a visible page and models one short opening sentence.
  4. Writers adapt the shared choice on their own pages with one different detail.

Quiet Partner Polish

Best for: Small groups with writers who can read one line to a partner or adult helper.

  1. Each writer chooses one sentence, sketch label, or dictated line to show a partner.
  2. The listener names one clear detail and asks one neutral question about the next action.
  3. The writer adds one noun, verb, or setting word based on the question.
  4. Adult closes the routine by asking everyone to mark one sentence they want to keep.

Station-to-Story Rotation

Best for: Longer club sessions with two or more tables and one adult leader plus helpers.

  1. Adult sets four stations: setting, helper, small mix-up, and ending choice.
  2. Groups spend four minutes at each station adding one note to their own page.
  3. After the final station, writers return to seats and order the four notes first, next, then, and last.
  4. Writers draft a short story using the ordered notes while adults help with spelling only when asked.

Folder Finish Reset

Best for: The last 8 minutes when a club needs a calm ending and clean handoff.

  1. Adult asks writers to stop at the end of the sentence they are working on.
  2. Writers mark one finished part with a star or underline and one unfinished part with a sticky note.
  3. Adult gives one next-step choice: add a title, add an ending, or add one clearer detail.
  4. Pages go into folders before supplies are collected and tables are reset.

Optional share prompts

  • Read one sentence that shows a clear setting detail.
  • Point to one sketch, title, or word bank item and tell how it helps the story.
  • Ask an adult to read one line while you name the word you chose carefully.
  • Share one revision move you made: add, move, replace, or explain.
  • Tell the group one next action your invented character could take.
  • Pass today and listen for one clear detail in another writer's story.

Club tools

Extension activity menu

12 minutes | setting sequence

Shelf-Shape Setting Map

Writers draw a simple shelf, table, cart, or room shape and label three invented story stops with one calm detail at each stop.

8 minutes | main idea

Title Three Ways

Writers create three possible titles: one with a place, one with an object, and one with an action, then choose the clearest title.

10 minutes | story structure

Object-to-Helper Sort

Writers choose three ordinary objects from a printed list and sort them into setting detail, helpful item, and ending clue.

12 minutes | sequence

Quiet Action Ladder

Writers list three small actions a character could take, then number them in an order that makes the story easier to follow.

15 minutes | dialogue

Dialogue Without Names

Writers draft four short lines between two invented characters using role labels such as guide and maker instead of personal labels.

10 minutes | revision

Revision Sticky Move

Writers place a sticky note beside one sentence and choose one revision move: add, move, replace, or explain.

8 minutes | word choice

Read-Aloud Line Test

Adult reads one volunteer sentence aloud while the writer listens for a word to keep and a word to make clearer.

12 minutes | ending choice

Ending Choice Pair

Writers draft two possible endings for the same story: one that solves the mix-up and one that invites another small adventure.
