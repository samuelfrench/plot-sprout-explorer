Adult guide

Solution coaching

Before the session

  • Print the solution cards, blank slips, and adult guide before the adult-led paper session.
  • Use drawn paper clips or loose paper-clip shapes as story props, keeping every detail made-up and broad.
  • Keep every solution fictional, broad, offline, printable, and guided by an adult.
  • Choose a small stack so each writer works with one pretend problem and one fitting solution.
  • Remind writers that a solution can be revised when the problem fit becomes clearer.

Paper clip solution setup

  • Place a drawn paper clip at the center of the page with four labels: need, tool, step, result.
  • Let the adult read the card and point to the label that should start the solution.
  • Ask writers to invent one helper, one gentle problem clue, and one small solution step.
  • Use blank slips for alternate fixes so crossed-out ideas stay low-pressure.
  • Keep the paper clip broad: it can become a bridge, tag, pointer, clasp, handle, marker, or holder inside the story.

Story solution coaching

  • Ask what the problem needs before asking what the helper should do.
  • Keep the solution small enough for one character action and one visible result.
  • If the solution gets too large, shrink it to one clue connected by the paper clip.
  • Ask what changes after the first solution try so the next sentence has a purpose.
  • Use gentle revision by swapping one vague fix for a clearer invented action.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Use invented names, broad story labels, and pretend places only.
  • Keep the activity paper-only, offline, and adult-led.
  • Keep all details made-up and broad, using story labels instead of outside facts.
  • If a detail sounds personal, replace it with a made-up paper-clip label or story object.
  • Reading stays optional and limited to one fictional word, sketch, or line.

Family handoff

  • Pass along one chosen solution slip and one blank slip for another fictional fix.
  • Tell family adults to ask about the made-up problem and solution, not actual life details.
  • Invite praise for one helpful clue, one fitting action, or one revised solution word.
  • Keep the carry-along activity adult-led, offline, and paper-only.
  • Ask family adults to leave narrow personal facts off the paper and keep the story invented.

Reset

  • Collect unused cards, blank slips, and paper-clip props.
  • Recycle scratch paper that includes narrow real-world facts.
  • Save only fictional examples that can model gentle solutions later.
  • Restock the pack with fresh blank slips before the next paper session.

Use one paper clip solution card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Solution routines

Choose the paper clip solution rhythm

Clip Need Match

Writers who need to connect a problem need to one useful action.

  1. The adult names the pretend problem need from the card.
  2. The writer chooses one paper-clip job: hold, point, join, or mark.
  3. The adult asks how that job helps the made-up character take one step.
  4. The writer writes one solution sentence that matches the need.

Tiny Fix Ladder

Keeping solutions small enough for one short story scene.

  1. The adult draws three small rungs on a paper slip.
  2. The writer names a first try, a better try, and a result.
  3. The adult asks which rung is the smallest useful solution.
  4. The writer chooses that rung and adds one fictional clue.

Object Becomes Helper

Writers who like solving through an invented story object.

  1. The adult points to the drawn paper clip and asks what it could become in the story.
  2. The writer chooses one safe make-believe object job.
  3. The adult asks what problem clue the object touches or holds.
  4. The writer writes how the object helps without solving everything at once.

Problem-Fit Check

Testing whether a solution actually answers the card's problem.

  1. The adult rereads the problem clue from the card.
  2. The writer says which part of the solution matches that clue.
  3. The adult asks what would still be unclear after the first try.
  4. The writer revises one word so the solution fits better.

Kind Choice Swap

Keeping a fictional solution gentle and cooperative.

  1. The adult asks whether the first solution sounds too big, too sudden, or too bossy.
  2. The writer chooses one gentler action word.
  3. The adult asks how the new action still helps the made-up problem.
  4. The writer swaps the action word and keeps the rest of the solution.

Result Then Revise

Showing how a first solution can reveal the next clearer fix.

  1. The writer writes one result after the first solution try.
  2. The adult asks what clue the result reveals.
  3. The writer circles one word that can become clearer.
  4. The writer revises the solution with the new clue.
