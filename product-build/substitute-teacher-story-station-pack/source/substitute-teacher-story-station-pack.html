Substitute setup

Run the substitute stations

Before the day

  1. Print one routine page for each station you plan to use.
  2. Print enough early finisher cards for quiet choice work.
  3. Place pencils, blank paper, and the selected station pages in one folder.
  4. Choose three to five stations based on the time available.
  5. Leave a simple note telling students that every story world is invented.

Morning setup

  1. Set station pages where students can reach them without extra explanation.
  2. Put blank paper beside each station page.
  3. Post or read the station order before students begin.
  4. Keep early finisher cards in a separate quiet-choice stack.
  5. Use a visible timer if the group already follows timed rotations.

During stations

  1. Read the first direction aloud, then let students work independently or with a partner.
  2. Remind students to write about invented characters and places only.
  3. Encourage short, complete ideas instead of perfect spelling.
  4. Rotate when the group is ready, or let students finish one station deeply.
  5. Use early finisher cards for students who complete a station before the group moves on.

End of day

  1. Ask students to place finished pages in the class work stack or take-home folder.
  2. Collect unused station pages so the teacher can reuse them.
  3. Circle any station that students especially enjoyed.
  4. Note whether the group completed all planned rotations or only part of the set.
  5. Leave early finisher cards in the substitute folder for future use.

Handoff

  1. Students completed printable story station work from invented-world prompts.
  2. No digital tools, personal details, or online sharing were used.
  3. The strongest participation appeared during the station choices marked on the pages.
  4. Unused pages and quiet-choice cards are returned to the folder.

Station tools

Station routines and optional share prompts

Map the Tiny Kingdom

Best for: A calm first station when students need a clear starting point

  1. Draw a small invented kingdom with three places: a gate, a workshop, and a quiet garden.
  2. Label each place with one detail that could start a story.
  3. Choose one place and write what a friendly character notices there.
  4. Add one kind problem the character can solve before leaving the kingdom.

Message from Moonberry Mail

Best for: Pairs or independent writers who enjoy letter-style prompts

  1. Imagine a mail cart arrives from a town called Moonberry Hollow.
  2. Write the first three sentences of the message inside the envelope.
  3. Decide what helpful errand the message asks the reader to complete.
  4. Write a short reply explaining the first step of the plan.

Three Object Story Tray

Best for: Students who benefit from concrete choices before drafting

  1. Pick three invented objects: a glowing button, a silver seed, and a paper compass.
  2. Choose which object a character finds first.
  3. Write how the object changes the character's plan for the day.
  4. End with the character using one object to help a neighbor in the story world.

Quiet Quest Countdown

Best for: Short blocks, tutoring sessions, or a final station before cleanup

  1. Fold a page into four boxes and number them one through four.
  2. In box one, sketch a peaceful invented place.
  3. In boxes two and three, write two steps a character takes to complete a small quest.
  4. In box four, write one sentence showing how the place is better at the end.

Story Helper Circle

Best for: Small groups that need a structured way to build one shared idea

  1. Each student writes one invented setting on a scrap of paper.
  2. Choose one setting and place it in the center of the group.
  3. Each student adds one friendly helper, tool, or clue for that setting.
  4. Independently write a short scene using the shared setting and one added idea.

Optional share prompts

  • Read one sentence that shows where your story takes place.
  • Share one invented object and explain why it matters.
  • Tell the group one kind choice your character made.
  • Name one detail you added after your first draft.
  • Share a title and explain which story clue it gives.
  • Tell one next scene you could write if you had more time.
