Adult guide

Story ingredient coaching

Before the session

  • Print the story recipe cards, take-home slips, and adult guide before the session; every recipe word means a story choice only.
  • Set the adult-led rule: writers use paper, pencils, sketches, and imagination at the table.
  • Choose one card format before writers begin so the table has one clear path.
  • Say that a story ingredient can be a setting, character, object, sequence, sensory detail, or revision choice.
  • Keep the session offline, calm, and focused on one short card that can become a later story.

Table setup

  • Place blank recipe cards, take-home slips, and pencils where the adult can see every page.
  • Set six labeled piles: setting recipe, character recipe, object recipe, sequence recipe, revision recipe, and sensory recipe.
  • Put one broad story place card on the table, such as kitchen table, porch table, reading corner, library table, or classroom table.
  • Leave open space on each card for a title, three story ingredients, one sketch, and one sentence.
  • Keep unused cards in an adult folder so the writing surface stays simple and paper-only.

Story ingredient coaching

  • Ask for one setting ingredient first: a made-up place, a broad real place word, or a mood word.
  • Ask for one character ingredient next: a fictional role, a careful choice, or a small question.
  • Ask for one object ingredient that has a story job, such as helping, hiding, guiding, or reminding.
  • Ask for one sequence ingredient with first, next, and finally, with no need to hurry.
  • Ask for one revision ingredient that makes a sentence clearer by adding a look, sound, pattern, or feeling word.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Use invented names, role words, or blank labels for every person on the cards.
  • Use broad place words instead of addresses, exact schedules, group names, signs, or private routines.
  • Keep recipe words as story metaphors; do not turn the cards into real table directions.
  • Keep finished cards in the family folder, tutor folder, co-op folder, or classroom folder.
  • Check every take-home slip for private details before it leaves the adult-led table.

Family handoff

  • Send one finished story recipe card and one blank take-home recipe slip with each writer.
  • Tell family adults that five to nine quiet minutes is enough for one extra story recipe.
  • Invite the family adult to ask for one setting ingredient, one character ingredient, and one object ingredient.
  • Ask adults to praise one clear word, sketch, or sentence before suggesting one small revision.
  • Suggest keeping the cards together so the writer can reuse the strongest ingredient in a later printable story.

Reset

  • Collect unused cards, slips, pencils, and broad place cards.
  • Review finished pages for private details before they go home.
  • Recycle scrap pages that only hold practice marks.
  • Restock the adult folder with fresh paper cards for the next offline session.

Use one paper card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Card formats

Choose the paper card rhythm

Setting Recipe Card

Writers who need a clear place before choosing characters or objects.

  1. Adult places one broad place card beside the blank recipe card.
  2. Writer chooses a setting ingredient and writes it on the first line.
  3. Writer adds one look detail and one mood word to the card.
  4. Writer writes one sentence that shows where the story begins.

Character Recipe Card

Kids who enjoy inventing roles, wishes, and questions.

  1. Adult asks the writer to choose an invented character role.
  2. Writer adds one careful want, worry, or wonder to the card.
  3. Writer sketches one small clue about the character.
  4. Writer writes one sentence about what the character notices first.

Object Recipe Card

Families or tutors turning ordinary table ideas into story helpers.

  1. Adult asks the writer to name a made-up object or choose one from a printed idea list.
  2. Writer gives the object one story job.
  3. Writer adds one detail about how the object looks or sounds.
  4. Writer writes one sentence showing why the object matters.

Sequence Recipe Card

Writers who need a beginning path before drafting.

  1. Adult points to the first, next, and finally blanks on the card.
  2. Writer chooses one setting ingredient and one character ingredient.
  3. Writer fills the three blanks with calm story steps.
  4. Writer circles the step that would make the strongest first paragraph.

Revision Recipe Card

Writers strengthening a sentence they already started.

  1. Adult places an earlier sentence beside the blank revision card.
  2. Writer chooses one clearer look, sound, pattern, or feeling word.
  3. Writer copies the sentence and adds the new ingredient.
  4. Writer marks the one word that improved the sentence most.

Sensory Recipe Card

Quiet writers who notice sound, color, pattern, or texture words.

  1. Adult asks the writer to choose one safe paper-only sensory word.
  2. Writer pairs the word with a setting, object, or character.
  3. Writer sketches the detail in the card corner.
  4. Writer writes one sentence where the detail gives a story clue.
