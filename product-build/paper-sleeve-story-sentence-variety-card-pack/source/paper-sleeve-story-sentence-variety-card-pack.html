Adult guide

Story sentence-variety coaching

Paper Sleeve Story Sentence Variety Adult Guide

  • Print the paper sleeve sentence variety cards, blank strips, and guide pages before the adult-led paper session.
  • Begin with one plain fictional sentence, then coach short sentences, stretched sentences, question sentences, starter swaps, sentence combines, rhythm checks, and final lines.
  • Keep every example made-up, broad, printable, offline, paper-only, and guided by an adult.
  • Use one sleeve strip for each sentence shape so the writer can compare rhythm without crowding the page.
  • Change sentence shape without adding narrow outside facts; use invented objects, broad story places, and made-up character names.
  • End each activity by copying one final varied fictional sentence that sounds clear, smooth, and intentional on paper.

Use one paper sleeve story sentence-variety card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, paper-only, and screen-free.

Short Sentence Fold

Turning a crowded fictional idea into one clear short sentence.

Stretched Sentence Add-On

Adding one useful detail to a short fictional sentence.

  1. The adult places the short sentence beside a blank stretch strip: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses one useful detail about an invented object, action, or broad story place: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks where the detail fits without making the sentence crowded: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the stretched sentence with one added detail: ____________________.

Question Sentence Turn

Trying a question sentence to create curiosity on paper.

  1. The adult writes a statement from the fictional card: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses the wondering word that fits the sentence, such as what, why, how, or where: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks how the statement must change so it becomes a question: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the question sentence with the same made-up idea: ____________________.

Starter Swap Slide

Moving the sentence starter so two lines do not begin the same way.

  1. The adult writes two fictional sentences that begin in a similar way: ____________________.
  2. The writer circles the starter that can move to a new position: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks whether an action, object, or broad story place should start the revised line: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the starter-swap sentence on the sleeve strip: ____________________.

Sentence Combine Fold

Joining two tiny fictional sentences into one smoother sentence.

  1. The adult writes two short fictional sentences on separate strips: ____________________.
  2. The writer marks the shared idea that lets the two strips fold together: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which joining word or comma spot makes the combined line smooth: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the combined sentence as one clear line: ____________________.

Rhythm Check Final Line

Choosing the sentence shape that sounds best before finishing.

  1. The adult places the short, stretched, question, starter-swap, and combined options in a row: ____________________.
  2. The writer marks the sentence that has the best paper rhythm for the pretend moment: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks whether one word should move, change, or come out before copying the final line: ____________________.
