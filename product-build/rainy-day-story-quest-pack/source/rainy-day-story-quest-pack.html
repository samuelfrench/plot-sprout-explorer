Adult setup guide

Run the rainy-day session

Setup

  1. Print the pack single-sided so kids can spread pages out and point before writing.
  2. Choose one pencil, one crayon or highlighter, and one quiet spot near a window if possible.
  3. Keep the session offline with no accounts, no uploads, and no public sharing.
  4. Let the child skip one page and return to it after the final story draft.

Session flow

  1. 0-5 min | Pick the world: Read the cover and let the child choose the first world by pointing, circling, or saying the title aloud.
  2. 5-12 min | Map the route: Use the route map to choose where the hero starts, stops, and notices one weather clue.
  3. 12-22 min | Gather words: Complete the forecast, description, dialogue, or sentence page before asking for full paragraphs.
  4. 22-35 min | Draft the story: Move selected words and sentences onto the final story page in beginning, middle, and ending order.
  5. 35-45 min | Share and revise: Ask the child to read one favorite sentence and add one clearer detail, label, or speech line.

World menu

Ages 7-8

Teacup Town Weather Window

Inside a blue teacup, a tiny town checks the weather through a spoon-shaped window and plans errands around drizzle, sunbeams, and toast-scented fog.

Ages 8-10

Rain Gauge Railway

A tiny railway crosses a backyard weather station, carrying raindrop samples, cloud notes, and garden alerts between labeled posts after every shower.

Ages 7-9

Spoon Ferry Lunchbox Harbor

At Lunchbox Harbor, spoon ferries carry menu cards, snack orders, and polite dockside dialogue between tiny cracker cafes.

Ages 7-9

Rain Boot Route Rangers

A pair of rain boots becomes a traveling route office whenever puddles appear, helping tiny shopkeepers choose dry paths for deliveries.

Support moves

  • Offer two choices instead of asking an open question when the child gets stuck.
  • Let the child draw or label first, then turn one label into a sentence together.
  • Write down the child's spoken sentence exactly once, then ask them to copy or improve one word.
  • Use a timer for a short writing burst, but stop before the page feels crowded.
  • Praise a concrete move such as a clear clue, polite dialogue line, or useful map label.

Extension ideas

  • Turn the forecast cards into a second story set on a sunny day.
  • Use a measuring cup or real rain gauge to add one true weather note.
  • Let two children write different endings from the same map.
  • Staple the final story behind the favorite planning page for a small portfolio piece.

Adult Guide: 35-Minute Session

Ask your adult to read the first step aloud.

Run the pages as short stations, not as a test.

Low-Pressure Order

1. Pick one world.

2. Point to a map spot.

3. Circle three useful words.

4. Write one speech line.

5. Finish one short story.

When Writing Slows Down

Prompt: Which clue helps the hero next?

Prompt: What would the helper say politely?
