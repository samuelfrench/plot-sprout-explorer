Facilitation notes

  • Start with the story question when the writer needs a clear theme path: ____________________.
  • Use the repeated clue to point back to the same idea without asking for a polished sentence: ____________________.
  • Ask for one character choice that shows what matters in the fictional story: ____________________.
  • Save the ending echo until the writer can connect the ending to the first story question: ____________________.
  • Let the theme line stay simple, plain, and adult-supported: ____________________.

Safety notes

  • Use invented characters, broad pretend settings, and made-up clues only: ____________________.
  • Keep the activity offline, paper-only, adult-led, and free from outside audiences: ____________________.
  • Skip any prompt that starts to ask for writer facts, group labels, or outside-session details: ____________________.
  • Avoid outside titles, creator names, service forms, labels from real shelves, or correction marks: ____________________.
  • Treat the theme line as a fictional story idea, not a claim about a real person: ____________________.

Use one story theme card at a time. Keep every theme line broad, invented, paper-only, and guided by an adult.
