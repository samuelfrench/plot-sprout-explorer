Adult guide

Story tone coaching

Sticky Note Story Tone Adult Guide

  • Print the sticky-note tone cards, blank revision slips, and adult guide pages before the adult-led paper session.
  • Begin with one neutral fictional line, then coach tone through word choice, object signal, place cue, character gesture, sentence frame, and revised sentence.
  • Keep every example made-up, broad, offline, printable, and guided by an adult.
  • Use one sticky note for each tone move so the writer can move, compare, and revise small pieces without crowding the page.
  • Replace narrow outside details with pretend names, broad story places, and invented objects before writing begins.
  • End each activity by choosing the revised sentence that makes the story feel calm, curious, cheerful, formal, playful, or gentle on the page.

Use one sticky note story tone card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Story tone routines

Choose the sticky note story tone rhythm

Neutral Line Lift

Starting tone work from a plain fictional sentence.

  1. The adult places one neutral line sticky at the top of the page: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses a safe tone word such as calm, curious, cheerful, formal, playful, or gentle: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which plain word could shift toward that tone: ____________________.
  4. The writer rewrites the line with one tone-matching word choice: ____________________.

Word Choice Swap

Changing the feel of a sentence without changing the story event.

  1. The adult circles one plain verb or describing word in the neutral line: ____________________.
  2. The writer lists two softer, brighter, quieter, or more formal word choices: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which choice best matches the intended tone: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the revised word choice in the sentence: ____________________.

Object Signal Sticky

Letting a pretend object carry part of the tone.

  1. The adult adds a blank sticky for one invented object signal: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses a gentle object action, such as shimmer, lean, fold, hover, or settle: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks what tone the object signal gives the moment: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes one object-signal sentence that keeps the line fictional: ____________________.

Place Cue Shift

Using a broad pretend place cue to support tone.

  1. The adult names the broad pretend place already in the neutral line: ____________________.
  2. The writer adds one place cue, such as a lantern edge, counter mat, shelf tag, paper arch, or table mark: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks whether the cue makes the tone feel calmer, brighter, quieter, or more formal: ____________________.
  4. The writer revises the sentence with the place cue included: ____________________.

Gesture Tone Pass

Showing tone through a fictional character's visible movement.

  1. The adult points to where a character gesture could fit in the neutral line: ____________________.
  2. The writer chooses one safe gesture, such as smoothing a note, tilting a tag, stepping back, or raising eyebrows: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks what tone the gesture gives the sentence: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the gesture into a revised tone sentence: ____________________.

Frame and Final

Turning separate tone choices into one finished sentence.

  1. The adult offers a sentence frame such as When the object moved, the character did something small: ____________________.
  2. The writer fills the frame with one tone word choice, one object signal, and one gesture: ____________________.
  3. The adult asks which extra word can be removed so the tone stays clear: ____________________.
  4. The writer writes the final revised sentence on the last sticky note: ____________________.
