Adult guide

Setting coaching

Before the session

  • Place one printed card stack, blank slips, and pencils in a shared paper tray before writers gather.
  • Read the pack aloud as pretend work; every setting must stay invented and broad.
  • Choose one adult caller to handle card turns, pencil supply, and calm table pacing.
  • Keep extra cards face down so the active prompt feels simple and paper-only.
  • Invite writers to mark slips with invented symbols only when a card needs a label.

Paper setting setup

  • Set a tray label that says Pretend Setting Cards and leave space for finished slips.
  • Sort cards by broad story-place type: sky, garden, tiny market, floating shelf, and invented festival.
  • Pair each card with one blank slip so writers can name a fictional detail.
  • Keep answer slips loose rather than bound, so the tray stays light and fictional.
  • Use only paper pieces, pencils, and printed cards for this offline lane.

Story setting coaching

  • Ask for sensory but invented clues: color, sound, texture, weather, and a small surprise.
  • If a writer names something actual, guide them to rename it with a made-up title.
  • Keep prompts broad: a moonlit pier works better than an overly narrow named spot.
  • Model one sentence that links setting to mood while staying gentle and family-friendly.
  • Praise clear paper choices instead of speed, ranking, or correctness.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Use invented labels and pretend symbols only.
  • Skip any prompt that starts to collect family routines or traceable clues.
  • Do not write actual names, map clues, travel paths, or narrow real-world facts.
  • Keep finished slips in the tray or send them with families; sharing stays adult-led and offline.
  • Adults should rewrite any actual-world clue into an invented broad story-place before discussion.

Family handoff

  • Send one selected slip with a short note inviting more pretend setting play at the family table.
  • Tell adults that blanks are for made-up details, not narrow real-world facts.
  • Suggest using a fresh paper tray at the next gathering to keep the activity offline and light.
  • Offer the optional prompts as aloud choices, not required written reports.
  • Invite families to keep or recycle slips after the story talk.

Reset

  • Collect unused cards, blank slips, and pencils into the paper tray.
  • Remove any slip that accidentally names a narrow real-world fact.
  • Shuffle fictional setting cards so the next group starts fresh.
  • Leave the tray empty of completed slips unless an adult intentionally sends them with a family.

Use one paper setting card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

setting routines

Choose the paper setting rhythm

Tray Flip Setting Start

Writers who need a low-pressure opening.

  1. Adult places three setting cards face up in the paper tray.
  2. Each writer chooses one card and adds one invented detail on a blank slip.
  3. Adult reads the slips aloud and asks which detail changes the story mood.
  4. Group keeps one slip under the card as the next story seed.

One Clue Many Worlds

Groups practicing flexible imagination.

  1. Adult names one gentle clue such as blue mist or silver moss.
  2. Writers each place that clue into a different fictional setting card.
  3. Adult invites each writer to add one texture or sound on paper.
  4. Group compares how the same clue feels different in each made-up story-place.

Weather Mood Swap

Linking setting to feeling without heavy themes.

  1. Adult selects one setting card and places two blank weather slips beside it.
  2. Writers invent two gentle weather choices for the same setting.
  3. Adult reads both choices and asks how each changes the opening line.
  4. Group circles one choice to use in a new paper story start.

Tiny Prop Big Setting

Writers who like concrete details.

  1. Adult places one pretend object slip beside a setting card.
  2. Writers describe where the object rests using only fictional clues.
  3. Adult asks what the object reveals about the wider story-place.
  4. Group writes one shared sentence that names the setting and the object.

Paper Gallery Pass

Quiet groups that benefit from turn-taking.

  1. Adult lays four fictional setting cards in a row on the table.
  2. Writers add one blank slip beside any card that gives them an idea.
  3. Adult reads the new slips in card order without naming writers.
  4. Group chooses one setting to carry into a short story start.

Setting Doorway Build

Starting a story with a strong entrance.

  1. Adult chooses a card and says the setting has an imaginary doorway.
  2. Writers draw or write one invented clue found near the doorway.
  3. Adult asks what the clue suggests about the setting beyond the doorway.
  4. Group writes one opening sentence that steps into the fictional story-place.
