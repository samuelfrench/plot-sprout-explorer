Adult guide

Start the thank-you note

Setup

  • Print the postcard pages, adult guide page, note situation cards, and revision prompt strips before the writing table opens.
  • Place pencils, blank scrap paper, and ordinary object idea slips within adult reach so writers can choose one small detail at a time.
  • Choose a simple recipient role for each note, such as helper, host, coach, tutor, neighbor, relative, or friend.
  • Invite writers to begin with a tiny invented story detail before drafting the real thank-you sentence.
  • Keep the session screen-free with printed cards, spoken choices, sketches, pointing, dictation, and short written lines.
  • Prepare one quiet restart page so a writer can switch ideas without explaining why the first draft felt stuck.

Coaching moves

  • Ask what kind action the recipient did, then help the writer add one invented object or tiny scene detail beside it.
  • Model one sincere sentence that names the kindness plainly and does not mention value, ratings, or comparisons.
  • Offer two concrete detail choices when a writer pauses, such as a blue mug or a paper star, and let the writer pick or invent another.
  • Guide the note through three parts: thank you, tiny story detail, and one warm closing sentence.
  • Keep corrections gentle by asking what the recipient should picture, hear, or understand more clearly.
  • Let writers share only a title, object clue, or one sentence if they want adult feedback.

Privacy notes

  • The pack is printable and offline; keep writing pages family-held.
  • Use role labels, initials chosen at home, or blank recipient lines.
  • Use role labels, initials chosen by the family, or blank recipient lines.
  • Keep finished notes with the household, tutor, or classroom adult; the product does not retain copies.
  • Stay with ordinary kindness, invented details, and simple objects instead of private adult or family details.

Family handoff notes

  • Send each writer home with the finished postcard draft and one optional revision strip.
  • Tell adults the note can be completed by pointing, dictating, sketching, or writing one short line.
  • Suggest reading the note aloud only if the writer chooses, with feedback focused on kindness and clarity.
  • Remind families that sending, delivery, or saving the note is handled outside the printable pack.
  • Offer one next step such as add a clearer object, add a sincere sentence, or copy the final version neatly.

Reset

  • Return unused situation cards and revision strips to the adult folder.
  • Recycle scrap pages that contain only practice ideas, unless the writer wants to keep them.
  • Restock blank postcard pages, sharpen pencils, and replace bent object slips.
  • Write only a nonpersonal supply note if needed, such as more pencils, fewer strips, or extra quiet-start pages.

Use one postcard at a time. Keep every story object invented, printable, and screen-free.
