Adult guide

Revision move coaching

Before the session

  • Print the notebook margin revision cards, take-home slips, and a small stack of blank draft pages.
  • Choose one fiction draft or one starter sentence before the writer sits down.
  • Set pencils, erasers, blank strips, and paper clips on the table so the whole routine stays screen-free.
  • Explain that margin notes are tiny helpful marks for made-up stories.
  • Pick one revision focus for the round: detail, word swap, order, setting, character, or ending.

Paper revision setup

  • Fold each draft page so the right side has a clear margin for adult-written prompts.
  • Keep the adult guide beside the paper stack and choose only one card at a time.
  • Use short margin marks such as add one detail, swap one word, stretch this sentence, or move this line.
  • Ask the writer to make the change on paper near the original line so the old and new versions stay visible.
  • Place skipped cards in a later pile without stopping the writing flow.

Story revision coaching

  • Treat each margin note as a choice, not a correction.
  • Read the original line, add one small change, then read both lines aloud.
  • Offer two concrete options when a writer freezes: a color or a sound, a shorter word or a stronger word.
  • Praise the visible change on the paper before inviting another change.
  • Stop after two or three useful changes so the draft still feels like the writer's story.

Privacy and safety notes

  • Keep every page offline in the folder chosen by the adult leader.
  • Use invented names, pretend places, and made-up notebook objects.
  • Redirect any personal-life sentence into a fictional detail before it goes on the page.
  • Do not require reading aloud; the adult can point to one revised word instead.
  • Skip any card that pulls the story away from gentle fiction.

Family handoff

  • Send one paper slip and one revised sentence home in a folder.
  • Tell family adults the task is one small fiction change, not perfect spelling.
  • Invite the adult at home to ask what changed in the margin note.
  • Suggest a five to nine minute table round with pencil and paper only.
  • Keep unfinished slips for a later adult-led session.

Reset

  • Collect unused cards, slips, pencils, and draft pages.
  • Return finished pages to the writer or adult folder.
  • Recycle scrap paper that only has warm-up marks.
  • Sort the card stack by revision focus before the next session.

Use one paper revision card at a time. Keep every choice broad, invented, and screen-free.

Revision routines

Choose the paper revision rhythm

Margin Dot Detail

Adding one clear picture detail to a plain line.

  1. Adult places a dot in the paper margin beside one plain sentence.
  2. Writer adds one color, shape, sound, or texture word near that line.
  3. Writer copies the sentence with the new detail below it.
  4. Adult asks which word made the picture clearer.

Word Swap Ladder

Replacing tired words without a long lesson.

  1. Adult underlines one simple word in the draft.
  2. Writer lists three possible swaps in the margin.
  3. Writer chooses one swap and rewrites only that sentence.
  4. Adult reads both versions and asks which one fits the made-up scene.

Sentence Stretch Strip

Writers with very short drafts.

  1. Adult draws a bracket around one short sentence.
  2. Writer answers who, where, or what else in the margin.
  3. Writer adds the answer to the sentence without changing the whole draft.
  4. Adult circles the added phrase so the stretch is visible.

Order Arrow Move

Drafts where events feel out of sequence.

  1. Adult writes first, next, finally on three small paper strips.
  2. Writer places one story sentence under each strip.
  3. Writer moves one strip to a clearer spot.
  4. Adult asks what linking word the moved line now needs.

Question In The Margin

Adding a next sentence when the story stalls.

  1. Adult writes one gentle question in the margin.
  2. Writer answers the question with one fictional detail.
  3. Writer turns that answer into the next story sentence.
  4. Adult draws an arrow from the question to the new sentence.

Ending Tuck-In

Drafts that stop suddenly.

  1. Adult brackets the last line and chooses one ending card.
  2. Writer adds one final object, sound, or choice in the margin.
  3. Writer writes a new closing line that uses the margin idea.
  4. Adult reads the old last line and the new last line.
