Teacher mini-unit

Tiny Errand Map Worksheet Sequence

Students plan a small helpful errand, draft a beginning-middle-end story, then revise with a reflection checklist.

Back to mini-units
Ages 7-9
Three 30-minute printable worksheet sessions
Lower elementary homeschool groups, tutoring pods, and classrooms working on short narrative planning.

Objectives

  • Choose a setting, helper role, and tiny errand before drafting.
  • Write a three-part story with a clear problem and helpful choice.
  • Use a revision checklist to add setting detail and reflect on one improved sentence.

Materials

  • World choice sheet
  • Tiny errand planner worksheet
  • Beginning-middle-end draft page
  • Revision and reflection checklist
  • Pencils and colored pencils

Lesson flow

30 minutes

Planner: Pick the Errand Path

Teacher move: Read the three world blurbs aloud, model choosing one helper role, and show how to circle a setting detail before writing.

Student task: Pick one world, choose a helper role, draw a simple errand path, and fill in who needs help, what is unclear, and what clue will guide the hero.

Output: Completed planner with a world choice, helper role, route sketch, and three story facts.

30 minutes

Draft: Build Three Story Steps

Teacher move: Demonstrate turning planner notes into three short paragraphs: arrival, mixed-up errand, and helpful ending.

Student task: Write a draft using the planner boxes, underline the problem sentence, and add one setting detail from the chosen world.

Output: One-page story draft with beginning, middle, and ending sections.

30 minutes

Revision: Check the Helpful Choice

Teacher move: Guide students through a read-aloud check for missing details, then model rewriting one sentence to make the action clearer.

Student task: Use the checklist to add one detail, fix one confusing sentence, and answer a reflection prompt about how the hero helped.

Output: Revised draft plus a short reflection note on the strongest change.

Teacher notes

  • Keep the story problem small: mixed-up notices, parcels, or route clues work better than large conflicts.
  • For reluctant writers, allow phrase lists in the planner before full sentences in the draft.
  • Use partner talk before writing, but keep the final worksheet individual for easier assessment.
  • If students finish early, have them add labels to the route sketch or write a new notice for the next helper.

Quest worlds used

  • Pocket Park Notice Board: A park small enough to fit in a jacket pocket uses a notice board for seed swaps, bench reservations, picnic lists, and cheerful cleanup reminders.
  • Paperclip Plaza Parcel Day: Paperclip Plaza holds Parcel Day every Friday, when tiny household-object shops swap neatly bundled notes, snack coupons, and map pieces.
  • Rain Boot Route Rangers: A pair of rain boots becomes a traveling route office whenever puddles appear, helping tiny shopkeepers choose dry paths for deliveries.

Homeschool and classroom use

Homeschool adaptation: Use the planner on day one, the draft page on day two, and the revision checklist on day three; a parent can scribe planner words while the child writes the final draft sentences.

Classroom management: Set out one worksheet at a time, keep world choices limited to the three listed options, and use a two-minute quiet check before sharing any draft sentence aloud.

Assessment: Check for a completed planner, a story with three ordered parts, and one visible revision that improves clarity or setting detail.
